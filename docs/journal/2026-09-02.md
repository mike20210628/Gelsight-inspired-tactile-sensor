## Design Decisions
I decided to route the LED wires through a slot on the upper side wall instead of drilling holes in the divider plate. The pre-soldered wires on the COB LED bars are very delicate and already broke once. If I routed them through the divider plate, the stiff wires would have to make a sharp 90-degree bend right at the solder joints, which would easily rip off the solder pads. An open slot on the outer wall lets the light bars drop straight in with zero strain on the wires.

I also kept the 5 mm middle divider solid. Leaving it closed makes the casing much stiffer when pushing down on the gel, and it stops stray light from the bottom camera chamber from leaking up into the dark sensing box.

I exported the CAD files in STEP format instead of STL or 3MF. STEP files preserve true round geometry for the screw and lens holes, avoiding the small polygon facets in STL files that make printed holes too tight.

---

## Observations
FDM 3D printers consistently print internal holes slightly smaller than designed (usually by 0.2–0.4 mm) because the hot plastic shrinks as it cools. Setting the clearance holes to 3.2 mm in CAD leaves just enough room for M2.5 screws to slide through easily without needing to drill them out by hand.

The pre-soldered joints on the LED strips are definitely the weakest mechanical point of the whole build. The case geometry has to fit around the wires rather than forcing the wires into tight spaces.

Standard 3D prints with only 2 wall layers let a tiny bit of light through when held up to a bright lamp. Increasing the wall count to 4 layers (about 1.6 mm solid plastic) makes the box completely light-tight, which is necessary for clean tactile imaging.
